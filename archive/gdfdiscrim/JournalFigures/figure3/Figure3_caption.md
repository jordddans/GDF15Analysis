**Figure 3 | A diagnostic classifier for PVOD built from plasma GDF15 alone, trained and evaluated on the present cohort.**

Logistic regression on log10(plasma GDF15), a single predictor, fitted to 166 patients — PVOD (n = 26) versus pooled IPAH (n = 98) and HPAH (n = 42) — with library default settings and no tuning, penalty, capping or flooring applied to the fitted model or its output. (a) Predicted probability of PVOD for every patient, grouped by true diagnosis; horizontal rules are group medians. Every probability shown is out-of-fold, obtained by leave-one-out cross-validation, so no patient contributed to the model that scored them. The dashed line marks the highest probability the classifier assigned to any patient (0.80). Cross-validated area under the ROC curve was 0.951 (95% CI 0.903–0.987); the in-sample value was 0.956. (b) Distribution of the decision cutoff across 4,000 stratified bootstrap resamples, drawn independently within the PVOD and the comparator groups so that group sizes are preserved. The cutoff was re-selected by the Youden criterion inside each resample, so the interval shown reflects the uncertainty of choosing the threshold as well as of estimating it. Bars are the distinct cutoff values the procedure selected — 13 in total, since with 26 PVOD cases a cutoff can only fall between adjacent observed values. The shaded band and arrow span the 95% interval (978–2,551 pg/mL); the point estimate was 1,659 pg/mL. The dashed line marks the 1,658 pg/mL cutoff published in the source study, which falls at the 36th percentile of this distribution. (c) Positive and negative predictive value at the point-estimate cutoff (sensitivity 0.81, 95% CI 0.69–0.96; specificity 0.97, 95% CI 0.72–1.00), plotted against assumed disease prevalence, with bands propagated from those intervals. PVOD comprises 15.7% of this cohort, a consequence of how the cohort was assembled rather than an estimate of prevalence in any clinical population; predictive values are therefore shown across a range of assumed prevalences rather than evaluated at a single one. All intervals are percentile bootstrap intervals from 4,000 resamples. Source data: Prabhakar et al., J Clin Invest 2026;136(2):e199159, Supplementary Source Data, sheet Fig_1a (panel Fig. 1a).
