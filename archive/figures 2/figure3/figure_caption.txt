Figure 3.  A GDF15-only diagnostic classifier for PVOD, built from the present cohort.

A binary logistic regression model was fitted with log10-transformed plasma GDF15 concentration as
the sole predictor and pulmonary veno-occlusive disease (PVOD, n = 26) versus pulmonary arterial
hypertension (PAH = IPAH + HPAH, n = 140; 166 patients in total) as the outcome. The model was
fitted by unpenalized maximum likelihood with default settings; no regularization, class weighting,
recalibration or variable selection was applied. Because the model is monotone in GDF15, its
decision rule is equivalent to a threshold on the concentration itself, and the cutoff was therefore
selected directly on the GDF15 scale by maximizing Youden's J (sensitivity + specificity − 1); where
several thresholds tie, the highest is taken. On the full cohort this gives 1,659 pg/mL, at which
the fitted model returns a probability of 0.32. Sensitivity, specificity and predictive values were
estimated by nested leave-one-out cross-validation: in each of the 166 folds the cutoff was re-
selected from the remaining 165 patients and then applied to the held-out patient, so that no
patient contributed either to the model or to the threshold used to classify them. The area under
the ROC curve is that of GDF15 itself and involves no fitted parameter. Uncertainty was estimated by
a stratified bootstrap (B = 2,000 replicates, resampling PVOD and PAH patients separately at their
observed sizes) in which the whole procedure, nested cross-validation included, was repeated within
each replicate; intervals are percentile intervals. Wilson score intervals are shown alongside them
for sensitivity and specificity.

(A) Plasma GDF15 for each of the 166 patients by diagnostic group (logarithmic axis); the right-hand
axis gives the probability of PVOD returned by the model fitted to the full cohort. The dashed line
is the full-cohort selected cutoff and the shaded band its bootstrap 95% confidence interval (978–2,551 pg/mL).
Open symbols mark the patients misclassified under nested cross-validation. (B) ROC curve for GDF15
with the pointwise bootstrap 95% band. The circle shows cross-validated sensitivity and specificity
from fold-specific cutoffs (bootstrap 95% intervals), not a single-threshold ROC operating point;
the square is the apparent operating point of the cutoff applied to the patients used to choose it. (C) Distribution of the Youden-selected
cutoff across the 2,000 bootstrap replicates, with its 95% percentile interval. (D) Cross-validated
sensitivity, specificity and predictive values at the selected cutoff, with bootstrap 95% percentile
intervals (coloured) and Wilson 95% intervals for sensitivity and specificity (grey); predictive
values are evaluated at the prevalence of PVOD in this cohort (15.7%), which is determined by the
sampling of the study. (E) Probability of PVOD after a positive and after a negative test result as
a function of the pre-test probability, with bootstrap 95% bands; the dotted line marks equality
between post-test and pre-test probability. (F) Calibration of the leave-one-out predicted
probabilities from the logistic model: observed proportion of PVOD within quintiles of predicted
probability (Wilson 95% intervals; quintile sizes given in the panel), the logistic recalibration
curve with its slope and intercept, and the dotted line of perfect calibration.

Source data: plasma GDF15 concentrations, one value per patient, from Figure 1a of this study.
